## Facility scale and cost

[Biffa: billion bottle milestone at its Washington/Sunderland plant](https://www.biffa.co.uk/biffa-insights/the-biffa-billion-bottle-milestone), article dated 11 March 2022, with a subsequent closure note. Reports £7m investment and 70 tonnes of post-consumer packaging processed daily. Feedstock was first sorted at Biffa sites across the UK; Washington cleaned and ground it into flakes. The same page states that the plant closed in 2024 because of market conditions for washed HDPE/PP flake.

Use as a historical process/scale example only, not a current operating plant, representative whole-city cost, annual capacity estimate or current-price quotation. It establishes why sorting, reprocessing, market demand and catchment must be distinguished. Do not scale construction costs linearly from throughput.

## Packaging policy and actual recycling

[PackUK RAM 2027 overview](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/assess-packaging-recyclability-recyclability-assessment-methodology-ram-2027/ram-2027-overview), updated 20 August 2026. Applies to the 2027 reporting year; the page directs 2026 assessments to RAM 1.1. Describes collection, sorting, reprocessing and subsequent use as distinct requirements, with operational capacity and actual performance relevant to assessment. A recyclability rating must not be presented as a guarantee that all packaging is collected or recycled.

[PackUK illustrative 2026 disposal fees](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/illustrative-waste-disposal-fees-2026-extended-producer-responsibility-for-packaging/year-2-illustrative-waste-disposal-fees-extended-producer-responsibility-for-packaging), updated 19 December 2025. Describes producer fees differentiated by recyclability. Its fee figures are explicitly illustrative, not a verified final November 2026 tariff. Suitable evidence for the incentive mechanism, not a ban on every poorly recyclable package or a quantified forecast of producer behaviour. If the puzzle assumes a change in material mix, supply it as a sourced forecast or explicit scenario assumption.

[England household Simpler Recycling guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/simpler-recycling-household-recycling-in-england) returned the original March 2027 film collection date. [LGA briefing of 17 July 2026](https://www.local.gov.uk/parliament/briefings-and-responses/flexible-plastic-recycling-briefing) reports a government intention to defer to April 2030. These sources conflict on timing/status. Do not publish either date as a settled legal requirement without checking the relevant legislation and latest official announcement. No date is needed for the initial conceptual proposal.

## Emissions and energy accounting

[UK 2026 GHG conversion-factor methodology](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a2940543b15d05a7ce3202e/2026-GHG-conversion-factors-methodology-report.pdf): search-extracted material-use guidance distinguishes purchased materials and warns about double counting production energy. Full methods have not been reviewed; corporate waste-reporting factors must not be assumed to equal whole-system avoided emissions.

[Scottish Government consultation cost-benefit analysis](https://consult.gov.scot/environment-forestry/a-new-household-recycling-code-of-practice/supporting_documents/cost-benefit-analysis-of-standardising-the-existing-code-of-practice-for-household-waste-recycling-in-scotlandpdf), appendix A.6, search-extracted: uses WRAP CarbonWARM2 and differentiates collection and treatment routes, including closed-loop glass versus aggregate. Relevant lead for methodological research; no numerical factor adopted here. The referenced [WRAP CarbonWARM2 v0.3 PDF](https://www.wrap.ngo/sites/default/files/2025-03/WRAP-CarbonWARM2.v0.3.pdf) could not be fetched by the web tool. A PackUK modulation-statement PDF also returned 410. No full review or usable cached PDF is claimed.

## Dataset requirements and publication limits

Fix an English city/catchment, reference year, material quantities and existing disposal routes. Compare compatible facility/process scopes, realistic capacities, capital and operating costs, recovered output and transport. Distinguish operating energy, energy produced and net energy avoided elsewhere. Calculate environmental benefit against one stated alternative with matching boundaries; include fixed burdens and avoid crediting the same material twice across sorting and reprocessing. Record source, price year, scope, uncertainty and rounding for each authored figure. Round only after confirming that realistic ranges preserve the intended deductions, or explicitly assess uncertainty.

No quantitative policy response, current statutory date or physical energy-saving total is asserted in these drafts.

Checks enumerate the cost-grid permutations and all facility pairs, derive cost and carbon figures from the ledger inputs, and test common wrong calculations. They establish arithmetic and internal consistency only. Actual completion time, source suitability for publication, site-specific feasibility and environmental uncertainty still require review.

## Fourth facility — 16 November 2026

[Wastewise Crewe, 26 May 2020](https://www.wastewise.co.uk/news/biowise-announces-7m-state-of-the-art-composting-facility-in-crewe-now-fully-operational/) reports £7m capital, 75,000 tonnes/year capacity and 45,000 tonnes/year initial throughput for mixed food/garden composting. Selected facts, retrieval date and derivations are cached in the ledger. ONS CPI 2020=108.7 converts this to £8.624m at the exercise's 2024 price level, rounded to £9m. The scenario assumes 20,000 tonnes of garden waste only. Defra Table 1 and PDF page 11 give garden landfill/composting factors of 579/72 kg CO₂e per tonne; their difference rounds to 0.5 tonnes CO₂e saved per tonne. The updated independent enumeration records six pairs, four affordable and one meeting both constraints; its sensitivity notes explicitly limit real-world interpretation.

## Electrical service — 16 November 2026

The longer activity now compares two food-only plans with a smaller food plant plus contracted electrical recycling. [Defra's 2023 WEEE impact assessment](https://consult.defra.gov.uk/product-regulation-and-producer-responsibility/consultation-on-reforming-the-producer-responsibil/supporting_documents/WEEE%20Review%20Impact%20Assessment%20Final%202023.pdf) supplies the actual electrical figures: Table 9 (PDF pages 40–41), £75/tonne net small-mixed-WEEE treatment in 2019 prices; Table 77 (page 83), consumption-based landfill/recycling factors of 9/−1,665 kg CO₂e/tonne. Selected facts, URL, retrieval date, scope and calculations are cached in the ledger; the full PDF is not cached. ONS 2019 CPI 107.8 adjusts the service allowance to the existing 2024 price basis. No current legal requirement is asserted from this consultation document. The assumed material mix, 5,000-tonne flow and reserved specialist capacity are explicit scenario inputs. Service fees are not treated as gross new-plant running expenses.
